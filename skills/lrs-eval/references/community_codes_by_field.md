# Community Codes by Field — Value Words and Instability Signals

Source: Larry McEnerney, "The Craft of Writing Effectively" (UChicago). Every disciplinary community has "code words" that signal value and instability. These words tell readers the paper matters. Using them correctly marks the writer as a community member; misusing or omitting them signals an outsider.

---

## How to Use This Reference

1. Identify the target audience/discipline of the paper under evaluation
2. Look up the community's value words and instability signals
3. Check whether the paper deploys these words in strategic positions (title, abstract, introduction, discussion)
4. Assess whether the words are used authentically (matching actual instability) or performatively (dropped in without substance)

---

## Epidemiology / Public Health

**Value words** (signal importance): risk, burden, disparity, inequity, determinant, prevention, intervention, population-level, modifiable, upstream, dose-response, attributable, exposure, incidence, prevalence, mortality, morbidity

**Instability signals** (mark problems): inconsistent findings, conflicting evidence, methodological limitations, confounding, selection bias, misclassification, reverse causation, ecological fallacy, unmeasured confounding, residual confounding, generalizability concerns, external validity, heterogeneous effects, differential misclassification

**High-value frames**: social determinants, health equity, translational, evidence-based, population attributable fraction, causal inference

## Cross-Disciplinary Note

Many papers span disciplines (e.g., sleep epidemiology in aging populations). In such cases:
1. Identify the **primary** target journal/audience
2. Prioritize that community's code words
3. Use secondary community codes where they strengthen the argument
4. Avoid code-switching mid-paragraph — pick one community's frame per section
