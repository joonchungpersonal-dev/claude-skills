# Nominalization Patterns — Reference for Sentence Clarity Analysis

Source: Joseph Williams, "Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace" (chapters on clarity). Nominalizations are nouns derived from verbs or adjectives. They drain prose of energy by hiding the action and obscuring the actor.

---

## Common Nominalization Suffixes

### -tion / -sion (most common in academic writing)
| Nominalization | Strong Verb | Example Fix |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| investigation | investigate | "The investigation of X" → "We investigated X" |
| examination | examine | "An examination of the data" → "We examined the data" |
| determination | determine | "The determination of risk" → "We determined the risk" |
| association | associate | "The association between X and Y" → "X was associated with Y" |
| implementation | implement | "The implementation of the protocol" → "We implemented the protocol" |
| administration | administer | "The administration of the survey" → "We administered the survey" |
| classification | classify | "The classification of participants" → "We classified participants" |
| consideration | consider | "Consideration of these factors" → "We considered these factors" |
| identification | identify | "The identification of risk factors" → "We identified risk factors" |
| interpretation | interpret | "The interpretation of results" → "We interpreted the results" |
| indication | indicate | "There is an indication that" → "The data indicate that" |
| exploration | explore | "An exploration of the relationship" → "We explored the relationship" |
| utilization | utilize/use | "The utilization of actigraphy" → "We used actigraphy" |
| collection | collect | "The collection of data" → "We collected data" |
| application | apply | "The application of criteria" → "We applied criteria" |
| estimation | estimate | "The estimation of effects" → "We estimated effects" |
| observation | observe | "The observation of differences" → "We observed differences" |
| contribution | contribute | "The contribution of sleep" → "Sleep contributes to" |
| reduction | reduce | "A reduction in risk" → "Risk was reduced" / "X reduced risk" |
| demonstration | demonstrate | "A demonstration of the effect" → "We demonstrated the effect" |

## Compound Nominalizations (especially problematic)

These chain multiple nominalizations, creating sentences where no one does anything:

| Compound | Fix |
|----------|-----|
| "The determination of the association between the identification of risk factors and the development of disease" | "We determined how identifying risk factors was associated with developing disease" |
| "An examination of the utilization of sleep measurement instruments" | "We examined how researchers use sleep measurement instruments" |
| "The implementation of the modification of the classification criteria" | "We modified and then implemented the classification criteria" |
| "Investigation of the contribution of the reduction in sleep duration" | "We investigated how reduced sleep duration contributes to..." |

## Density Benchmarks

| Density (per 100 words) | Rating | Description |
|--------------------------|--------|-------------|
| < 3 | STRONG | Clear, verb-driven prose |
| 3-5 | ADEQUATE | Some nominalizations but manageable |
| 5-8 | WEAK | Reader works too hard; prose feels heavy |
| > 8 | ABSENT (clarity) | Impenetrable bureaucratic prose |

**How to measure**: Count words ending in -tion, -sion, -ment, -ence, -ance, -ity, -ness in a representative passage (e.g., first paragraph of each section). Divide by word count, multiply by 100.

**Caveat**: Not all -tion words are nominalizations. Some are legitimate nouns (e.g., "population," "proportion," "condition," "equation," "institution"). The agent should distinguish between nominalized verbs (action hidden in a noun) and genuine nouns.

---

## When Nominalizations Are Acceptable

Nominalizations are not always wrong. They are acceptable when:

1. **The nominalization IS the topic**: "The investigation took three years" — the investigation itself is the subject, not a buried action
2. **Referring to a previous action**: "This analysis revealed..." following a paragraph that described how you analyzed
3. **Technical terms**: "regression," "classification," "stratification" — field-standard terms
4. **Shortening familiar concepts**: "sleep deprivation" is clearer than "depriving people of sleep" when it's an established construct
5. **In the stress position**: "The most important factor was patient adherence" — the nominalization carries emphasis

The diagnostic question: Is the nominalization hiding an action that would be clearer as a verb? If yes, fix it. If the nominalization is the topic of discussion, keep it.
