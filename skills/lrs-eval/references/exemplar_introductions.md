# Exemplar Introductions — Annotated Models

These are composite exemplar introductions demonstrating LRS principles. Each paragraph is annotated with the rhetorical move it performs. These are teaching examples, not from published papers.

---

## Exemplar 1: Sleep Epidemiology (Target: SSM — Population Health)

> Short sleep duration is consistently associated with elevated cardiometabolic risk across large prospective cohorts, with meta-analyses reporting 12-15% increases in incident diabetes and 6-8% increases in cardiovascular mortality for adults sleeping fewer than 6 hours per night (Cappuccio et al., 2010; Itani et al., 2017). **[STASIS — establishes what the community accepts; uses community codes: "cardiometabolic risk," "prospective cohorts," "incident," "meta-analyses"]** These findings have informed clinical guidelines recommending 7-9 hours of sleep for adults, now endorsed by multiple professional societies. **[STASIS EXTENSION — shows the stakes by connecting evidence to action]**
>
> However, the populations underpinning these recommendations are overwhelmingly urban, insured, and voluntarily enrolled — characteristics that correlate with health literacy, clinic access, and stable work schedules. **[DESTABILIZATION — sharp "however" pivot; identifies the instability as a structural bias, not merely a gap; uses instability signal "overwhelmingly" to quantify the problem]** Rural Appalachian communities, where rotating shift work, multi-generational caregiving, and provider scarcity are normative, remain absent from this evidence base. **[DESTABILIZATION CONTINUED — specifies who is missing and why it matters structurally]**
>
> This absence has concrete clinical consequences: the 60 million Americans living in rural communities receive sleep recommendations derived from populations whose structural circumstances differ fundamentally from their own. **[COST — specific, concrete, attributed to identifiable reader community (rural clinicians and their patients); uses "concrete clinical consequences" as community code; passes the McEnerney test: "Because of this problem, clinicians in rural settings are currently recommending interventions that may not work for their patients"]** If cardiometabolic associations with short sleep are attenuated or absent in populations where sleep restriction is structurally imposed rather than behaviorally chosen, current guidelines may be not merely inapplicable but actively misleading. **[COST ESCALATION — raises the stakes from "we don't know" to "we might be causing harm"]**
>
> We therefore examined associations between objectively measured sleep duration and cardiometabolic risk markers in the REST-KY cohort, a population-based sample of 1,200 adults from eastern Kentucky — a region with among the highest rates of cardiometabolic disease and the lowest access to sleep medicine in the United States. **[RESOLUTION — directly addresses the destabilization; specifies method (objectively measured), population (REST-KY), and scale (1,200); final clause reinforces why this specific population matters]**

**LRS Scorecard for this exemplar:**
- P01 Value to Readers: STRONG — explicitly frames value for clinicians serving rural populations
- P02 Problem Construction: STRONG — full Stasis → Destabilize → Cost → Resolution
- P03 Community Codes: STRONG — "cardiometabolic," "prospective cohorts," "meta-analyses," "incident," "population-based"
- P04 Instability over Gaps: STRONG — structural bias (instability), not "no one has studied rural sleep" (gap)
- P10 Topic Position: STRONG — each sentence begins with old information from prior sentence
- P11 Stress Position: STRONG — key new information lands at sentence ends ("absent from this evidence base," "actively misleading")
- P21 Costs to Readers: STRONG — specific readers (rural clinicians) bearing specific costs (wrong recommendations)

## Exemplar 2: Cognitive Psychology (Target: Journal of Experimental Psychology)

> Working memory capacity reliably predicts complex cognitive performance across domains, from reading comprehension (Daneman & Carpenter, 1980) to fluid intelligence (Engle et al., 1999). **[STASIS — core finding the community accepts]** Individual differences in working memory are typically attributed to executive attention — the ability to maintain goal-relevant information in the face of distraction (Engle, 2002). **[STASIS — dominant theoretical account]**
>
> Yet this account assumes that capacity limitations are stable within individuals across time and context. **[DESTABILIZATION — challenges a hidden assumption; uses "yet" signal; the instability is in the assumption, not in a gap]** Recent within-person designs reveal substantial trial-to-trial variability in working memory performance — variability that the executive attention framework neither predicts nor explains (Schmiedek et al., 2013). **[DESTABILIZATION CONTINUED — provides evidence for the instability; names what the dominant theory cannot do]** If capacity fluctuates meaningfully within individuals, between-person studies may be measuring a mixture of trait capacity and state variability, conflating two distinct sources of variance. **[COST — threatens the inferential basis of a large literature; specific to researchers who rely on between-person WM measures]**
>
> We used experience sampling to measure working memory performance 10 times daily for 14 days in 150 adults, decomposing total variance into stable trait and fluctuating state components. **[RESOLUTION — method directly addresses the destabilization; scale and design are foregrounded]**

**LRS annotations:**
- Destabilization targets a **hidden assumption** — more powerful than targeting a gap
- Cost is to **researchers** (their inferential framework is threatened), not to "the field"
- Resolution promises **decomposition** — the exact operation needed to resolve the instability

## Exemplar 3: Sociology (Target: American Sociological Review)

> The intergenerational transmission of educational advantage is one of the most robust findings in stratification research, with parental education consistently predicting children's attainment across industrialized nations (Blau & Duncan, 1967; Hout & DiPrete, 2006). **[STASIS — foundational finding; uses community codes: "intergenerational transmission," "stratification," "attainment"]** Causal mechanisms are typically theorized through three channels: economic resources, cultural capital, and social capital (Bourdieu, 1984; Coleman, 1988). **[STASIS — dominant theoretical framework]**
>
> However, these mechanisms assume that parental education translates into advantage through the same processes regardless of local institutional context. **[DESTABILIZATION — challenges universality assumption]** In communities where the dominant labor market does not reward educational credentials — such as extractive economies in Appalachia, where mining wages historically exceeded returns to education — the signaling value of parental education is structurally undermined. **[DESTABILIZATION — provides the specific mechanism of failure; uses instability signal "structurally undermined"]**
>
> If transmission mechanisms are context-dependent, the policy implication is significant: investments in parental education may not generate expected intergenerational returns in communities where the local opportunity structure does not convert credentials into mobility. **[COST — specific to policymakers investing in education; threatens the basis of a common policy intervention]** Programs designed with urban labor markets in mind may misallocate resources in regions where the education-to-earnings link is attenuated. **[COST CONTINUED — makes the cost tangible: wasted resources]**
>
> We test this proposition using three waves of the Appalachian Young Transition Study (N = 2,400), comparing intergenerational education transmission in counties with high vs. low extractive-economy dependence. **[RESOLUTION — directly tests the destabilization; longitudinal design appropriate for intergenerational claims]**

**LRS annotations:**
- Topic strings: "intergenerational transmission" → "these mechanisms" → "transmission mechanisms" → consistent string
- The instability is about **when the established finding breaks down**, not about a population no one studied
- Cost names a **specific policy failure** with a **specific geographic scope**

## Exemplar 4: Public Health / Implementation Science (Target: Implementation Science)

> Evidence-based insomnia interventions, particularly Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), demonstrate efficacy comparable to pharmacotherapy with more durable effects (Mitchell et al., 2012; Trauer et al., 2015). **[STASIS]** Professional organizations including the American College of Physicians now recommend CBT-I as first-line treatment (Qaseem et al., 2016). **[STASIS EXTENSION — institutional endorsement]**
>
> Despite this consensus, fewer than 1% of adults with insomnia receive CBT-I (Koffel et al., 2018). **[DESTABILIZATION — stark numerical contrast with "despite this consensus"; the instability is between what we know works and what actually happens]** The gap between evidence and practice is typically attributed to workforce shortages — too few trained CBT-I providers to meet demand. **[SETS UP SECOND DESTABILIZATION — names the standard explanation to challenge it]** But workforce explanations locate the problem in supply while ignoring demand: qualitative evidence suggests that many patients offered CBT-I decline it, preferring faster-acting pharmacological options even when informed of long-term risks (Cheung et al., 2019). **[SECOND DESTABILIZATION — challenges the dominant explanation with a different mechanism]**
>
> If the implementation failure is partly demand-side rather than exclusively supply-side, the dominant policy response — training more providers — will not close the evidence-practice gap on its own. **[COST — directly threatens a major policy investment; specific to implementation scientists and health system administrators]**
>
> We conducted a mixed-methods implementation study in three VA medical centers, examining patient decision-making at the point of insomnia treatment referral to identify modifiable demand-side barriers to CBT-I uptake. **[RESOLUTION]**

**LRS annotations:**
- Uses a **double destabilization** — first establishes the practice gap, then challenges the standard explanation
- The second destabilization is more powerful because it says "the field is not just failing to act — it is failing to act for the wrong reason"
- Cost threatens a **specific policy investment** (provider training programs)

## What Makes These Work (Summary)

1. **Stasis is brief and specific** — not a comprehensive literature review
2. **Destabilization names an instability** — not a gap
3. **Cost is personal** — readers can finish "Because of this problem, I am currently..."
4. **Resolution directly addresses the destabilization** — method matches the problem
5. **Sentence-level craft supports the rhetoric** — topic/stress positions, strong verbs, coherent paragraph structure
6. **Community codes appear in strategic positions** — title, abstract, first and last sentences of introduction
